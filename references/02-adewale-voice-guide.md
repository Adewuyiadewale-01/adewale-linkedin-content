# Adewale Voice Guide

> Canonical source for how Tony's LinkedIn content should sound, feel, and read.

**Last updated:** 2 September 2026

## Desired Voice

The writing should feel:

- Human, direct, intelligent, and curious
- Confident where evidence exists and honest where it does not
- Specific enough to demonstrate real experience
- Conversational rather than corporate
- Energetic without sounding manufactured
- Opinionated when Tony has a defensible argument
- Respectfully forceful when challenging a weak belief

## Stylistic Influences

### CJ Zafir: primary builder influence

Use CJ's accessible builder-led progression for projects, experiments, and personal posts:

- Begin with a genuine problem, preference, or observation.
- Introduce what Tony built or is testing.
- Provide enough technical detail to establish competence without excluding non-technical readers.
- State what currently works, what remains uncertain, and what comes next.

Improve on the reference style by defining necessary jargon, tightening loose sentences, and connecting the build to a clear consequence.

### Termsheetinator: primary analytical influence

Use Termsheetinator's structured reasoning for problem, systems, teardown, and commercial posts:

- State the problem or opportunity clearly.
- Explain the mechanism step by step.
- Include economics, constraints, assumptions, or operational consequences when available.
- Separate immediate effects from second-order implications.
- End with a clear strategic conclusion.

Depth should earn its length. Do not make every post dense merely to appear authoritative.

### Yegor: limited conviction influence

Borrow:

- Decisiveness
- Memorable phrasing
- Momentum
- Willingness to make a strong claim

Do not borrow:

- Personal insults
- Recklessness presented as wisdom
- Excessive profanity
- Manufactured dominance
- Constant capitalization
- Absolutism unsupported by evidence

Tony's preferred blend is accessible builder storytelling, analytical depth when warranted, and controlled conviction.

## Formatting Defaults

- Use short, readable paragraphs suited to the LinkedIn feed.
- Let paragraph length vary naturally rather than forcing every sentence onto a separate line.
- Use whitespace to clarify the argument, not manufacture suspense.
- Use lists only when the idea genuinely contains a sequence, comparison, or set.
- Keep emojis rare and purposeful unless Tony establishes a different preference.
- Use capitalization and punctuation naturally. Avoid theatrical formatting.
- Keep hashtags minimal or omit them unless they serve a specific purpose.
- Do not attach a question or call to action to every post by default.
- Match post length to the substance. Do not inflate a simple idea.
- Do not use em dashes.

## Structural Preferences

Select a structure based on the material rather than forcing every post into one template:

- Observation → explanation → implication
- Problem → mechanism → practical solution
- Build story → decision → result → lesson
- Mistake or failed assumption → discovery → changed approach
- Strong claim → evidence → nuance → conclusion
- Tutorial → steps → caveat → application

## Drafting Principles

Every strong post should ideally contain at least three of these:

- A concrete problem, observation, event, or personal moment
- A clear point of view
- Specific evidence, example, mechanism, or technical detail
- Something useful the reader can apply
- A memorable conclusion or genuine question
- A reason the insight belongs specifically to Tony

The opening should earn attention without misleading the reader. The body should develop one central idea. The ending should complete the thought before asking for engagement.

## Respectful Contrarian Standard

Criticism can be brutal in clarity but must remain respectful in delivery:

- Attack the belief or mechanism, not the person's dignity.
- Explain why the claim is incomplete or wrong.
- Use evidence, examples, or technical reasoning.
- Acknowledge important nuance.
- Do not soften a defensible conclusion merely to avoid disagreement.

## Avoid

- Generic motivational filler
- Empty AI hype or exaggerated certainty
- Invented experience, metrics, or outcomes
- Engagement bait that weakens credibility
- Over-polished language that no longer sounds like Tony
- Unnecessary jargon
- Formulaic hooks used only because they are common on LinkedIn
- Technical claims that collapse under basic scrutiny

## Adaptation Rules

- Tony's direct feedback outranks generic LinkedIn conventions.
- A one-off instruction remains specific to that post unless Tony confirms it should generalize.
- Repeated feedback becomes a candidate standing rule.
- Approved examples are stronger voice evidence than abstract descriptions.
- Record disliked examples with the exact reason they failed.
- Ask before turning an inferred preference into a permanent rule.
- Performance may influence presentation, but it must not erase Tony's authentic voice.
