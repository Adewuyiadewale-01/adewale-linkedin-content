# Adewale Content System

> Canonical source for subject areas, post formats, idea generation, and content development.

**Last updated:** 2 September 2026

## Three-Layer Architecture

1. **Subject areas** define what Tony discusses.
2. **Post formats** determine how an idea is presented.
3. **Idea sources and angles** keep the pipeline supplied.

An idea becomes stronger when all three are identified before drafting.

## Core Subject Areas

1. Backend systems
2. AI and workflow automation
3. Business operations and efficiency
4. Websites, products, and digital experiences
5. Building and developing as a technical professional

### Cross-audience bridge: systems behind digital experiences

Use this territory to explain the backend, automation, data, and operational machinery behind forms, dashboards, bookings, checkouts, customer portals, and other interfaces. It connects FMP's design/business audience with Adewale's technical audience.

## Confirmed Post Formats

### 1. Problems and better systems

Identify a recurring operational or technical problem, show what it costs or prevents, and explain the system or workflow that would solve it.

Suggested progression:

1. Name the visible problem.
2. Reveal the deeper mechanism.
3. Explain the current cost, risk, or limitation.
4. Design the improved workflow or system.
5. Clarify what should be automated and what still needs a person.
6. Address constraints and ongoing costs.
7. Conclude with the operational or commercial implication.

Example: a company repeatedly prompting ChatGPT to create recurring factsheets or reports instead of building a reusable workflow. Automation may still consume model tokens, but it can reduce repeated prompting, manual time, inconsistent outputs, missing validation, and disconnected distribution.

Default influence: Termsheetinator.

### 2. Projects built or being built

Discuss shipped work or work in progress through the problem, architecture, decisions, constraints, results, failures, and next steps.

A single project can generate:

- The original problem
- The proposed architecture
- A difficult technical decision
- A work-in-progress demonstration
- A failed approach or debugging discovery
- The finished build
- Results or limitations
- A retrospective after real use

Default influence: CJ.

### 3. Personal builder moments

Begin with a real moment such as a photograph of Tony's screen while coding, designing a workflow, debugging, researching, or thinking through a system. The image is evidence and context, not the entire idea.

Connect it to:

- A discovery
- A difficult decision
- Something Tony has been stuck on
- The contrast between a simple output and complex implementation
- An honest observation about learning or building
- A personal quality revealed through the work

Default influence: CJ with limited Yegor-like conviction.

### 4. Respectful contrarian criticism

Challenge a weak claim, lazy assumption, or harmful narrative directly without attacking people.

Suggested progression:

1. State the claim being challenged.
2. Give Tony's clear position.
3. Explain the mechanism that makes the popular belief incomplete or wrong.
4. Provide examples or evidence.
5. Add the necessary nuance.
6. End with the sharper, more accurate conclusion.

Example: AI has not made web designers irrelevant; it makes capable designers faster while exposing those whose value consisted only of slow production.

Default influence: controlled Yegor supported by CJ-like explanation.

### 5. Technical explainers and mental models

Teach how a backend, automation, or AI-system concept works using concrete examples. The primary goal is understanding, even when no project or business solution is being presented.

Possible subjects:

- APIs versus webhooks
- State management
- Duplicate automation runs and idempotency
- Scheduled jobs versus agents
- Databases, queues, and workers
- Validation and human approval
- Reliability and production readiness
- Why prompting cannot repair poor source data

Default influence: CJ's accessibility with Termsheetinator's structure.

### 6. Teardowns and audits

Examine an existing workflow, website journey, internal process, automation, or backend design without needing to have built it.

Explain:

1. What currently happens
2. Where the bottleneck or failure lies
3. What the user or business experiences
4. What should be automated
5. What still needs human judgment
6. How Tony would design the improved system
7. What outcome the redesign should produce

Default influence: Termsheetinator.

## Recurring Idea Sources and Angles

- A problem Tony notices
- Something Tony is building or has shipped
- A technical or business question someone asks
- A mistake, failure, debugging discovery, or changed decision
- A new model, product, feature, research result, or industry event
- A claim Tony agrees or disagrees with
- A workflow, website, backend, or business process worth auditing
- A personal moment captured while working
- A connection between design, operations, automation, and backend systems

News is an idea source, not a standalone format. Interpret what actually changed, what remains hype, what builders or businesses can now do differently, and what Tony intends to test.

Failures and changed decisions usually belong inside project posts, personal posts, or technical explainers rather than forming a separate main format.

## Idea Development Card

Before drafting, answer only the questions relevant to the post:

- What triggered this idea?
- Which subject area does it belong to?
- Which post format fits it best?
- Who should care most?
- What is the single central claim?
- What evidence, mechanism, or experience supports it?
- What would make the claim more nuanced or technically accurate?
- What useful implication should the reader leave with?
- What image, screenshot, diagram, or example would improve it?
- Is this one post or the beginning of a sequence?

## Content Reuse Principle

Do not repeat the same post. Revisit the same underlying material from a genuinely different question, stage, audience, or evidence base. One project or release can support problem, build, personal, explainer, contrarian, and teardown posts when each contributes a distinct idea.
